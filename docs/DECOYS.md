# Decoys in Cold Waters MP

Submarines in Cold Waters can carry mobile decoys: the American MOSS and the Soviet MG-74 Korund and older MG-14. A decoy is a small torpedo-shaped device that swims away from your sub and makes noise like a submarine. In single player it mostly distracts enemy torpedoes. Against a human opponent, the mod makes it a real tool for deception.

## What the other player sees

When you launch a decoy, your opponent's sonar picks up a second submarine of your class. It sounds like you would sound at the decoy's speed. It gets its own contact number, it can be classified, and it shows on their map like any other contact.

The decoy is quiet for the first 15 seconds, while it swims clear of your hull, and then gets louder over about 20 seconds. A MOSS or MG-74 runs at 20 knots for about 20 minutes, the MG-14 at 15 knots for about 17 minutes. Then it fades out.

Your opponent cannot shoot it down or sink it. Their torpedoes can be fooled by it, just like in single player.

## How to tell a decoy from the real sub

A decoy is a good imitation, but not a perfect one. A careful player can catch it:

- **Course and speed.** A decoy runs straight at one speed, usually faster than a sub trying to stay quiet. It never turns, slows down or reacts.
- **The signature.** In the ID book, one line in a decoy's signature sits slightly out of place compared to the real class.
- **No sounds of its own.** A decoy never launches anything and never makes a transient.
- **Running a torpedo past it.** If your torpedo goes for a contact and passes it without a hit, your crew reports it as a decoy.

If you suspect a contact, select it and press the DECOY button in the ID book (or Ctrl+F5). The contact is then marked "decoy?" on your map. This is only your own note: the game doesn't check it. If you later hit a contact you marked, the mark comes off and your crew tells you it was the real sub.

## Contact numbers

In single player, every new contact gets the next number (Sierra 1, Sierra 2 and so on) and keeps it forever. In a duel that would give decoys away: a fresh number appearing right where your opponent's sub was can only be a decoy. So the mod handles contact numbers a little differently.

**A decoy heard right next to a contact you are tracking** may take over that contact's number. Say you are tracking the enemy as S1 and a decoy starts up right beside it. Your crew calls a new contact, S2. But a coin decides which of the two is now called S1: the real sub, or the decoy. Your earlier classification and solution go with the S1 label. You have to work out from course, speed and signature which one is real.

**A contact you lose** keeps its number for a short while. Once your solution on it has faded (it would show as a plain circle on the map), the number is gone and a faded marker such as "S1 Barbel?" stays at its last known position.

**A new contact near that marker** takes the old number back, and your crew calls it regained. That works for the real sub coming back, and for a decoy launched after you lost the sub. Either way, the marker alone doesn't tell you which one you have.

**The faded markers** work like the game's own nav markers: shift-click to delete one. Once deleted, a new contact in that area gets a new number.

## Tips

**When you launch a decoy:**
- Fire it while the enemy is tracking you, then change course and speed and go quiet. They now have two contacts, and they may follow the wrong one.
- Fire it after you've slipped away. When they pick it up, it looks like you coming back.
- Send it in a believable direction. A "sub" that runs away at 20 knots in a straight line is suspicious.

**When you are hunting:**
- Watch what each contact does. A real sub changes course, slows down and goes quiet. A decoy doesn't.
- Compare signatures in the ID book.
- If you have torpedoes to spare, a torpedo run past a contact tells you what it is.
